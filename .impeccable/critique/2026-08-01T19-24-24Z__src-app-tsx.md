## Design Specificity Verdict

The clinical-pastel system is coherent, but category-interchangeable: aside from the logo and copy, it could market any summary platform. It lacks product proof, real study artifacts, and a distinct medical-student moment.

The deterministic scan was clean: 0 findings across the target markup. Semantic sections/articles, image alt text, decorative icon treatment, FAQ expansion state, and keyboard focus styling are present. Browser visualization could not be performed because no browser instance was available; no overlay or console evidence exists. There were no false positives.

## Overall Impression

The page is calm, trustworthy, and clear at the top. It earns confidence through its anti-hype voice, but asks a visitor to believe in an invisible product and repeats its core claim too many times before the purchase decision.

## What’s Working

- The hero makes the free sample the obvious low-risk first action and supports it with credible microcopy.
- The calm palette, generous spacing, and direct student language fit a stressed medical-student audience.
- Replacing fabricated testimonials with early-access messaging protects trust.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] No product proof

**Why it matters:** Five icon cards explain benefits but do not show what the visitor will access. The page sells an invisible platform.

**Fix:** Put a real, annotated 8Book/material preview directly near the hero or free sample. Show how topics, summaries, and questions are organized.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable bolder` or `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] Trust and authorship remain abstract

**Why it matters:** “Veteranos do 8º período” repeats, but there are no concrete provenance signals or sample material to substantiate it.

**Fix:** Add a restrained authorship/provenance module with real details that are approved to disclose, plus a sample page preview.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] Pricing creates unnecessary interpretation work

**Why it matters:** “Hoje”, “Primeiro ciclo”, renewal amount, equivalent monthly price, and period appear together. The visitor must calculate what is charged and when.

**Fix:** Use one plain cadence sentence per plan: “R$ 40 hoje; depois R$ 50 a cada 3 meses.” Explicitly label renewal and explain why each interval is the choice.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Mid-page narrative repeats itself

**Why it matters:** Hero, pain, value, authority, and first-access sections repeatedly say organization, veterans, and less chaos. This slows the route to pricing.

**Fix:** Merge value, authority, and early access into a tighter proof block, or replace one with product evidence.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P2] Mobile conversion does not continue down the page

**Why it matters:** The page is long, with only hero CTAs and plan CTAs. A mobile visitor reaching the FAQ ends without a clear next step.

**Fix:** Add a mobile sticky “Testar grátis” action and a final CTA after the FAQ.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-timer):** Can find “Testar gratuitamente” quickly, but cannot preview the sample’s content before leaving the page. “8Book” and “Locus Med” need a more persistent relationship label. There is no visible support route if checkout/access differs from expectation.

**Riley (stress tester):** Promises such as progress visibility and device access lack on-page proof or availability qualifiers. Pricing mixes introductory and renewal terms without a concise plan-specific contract sentence.

**Casey (distracted mobile user):** Large hero buttons are touch-friendly, but their effect is lost across the long page. Dense pricing and FAQ content increase interruption cost, and no end-of-page CTA restores the decision.

## Minor Observations

- Nearly every section uses the same border/card/pastel grammar; vary the composition to create milestones.
- Add top-level links to the sample and FAQ.
- Differentiate plans beyond billing interval.
- External-link icons on CTAs advertise departure at a commitment moment.
- The `Testimonials` component name is stale internally, even though the visible content is appropriate.

## Questions to Consider

- What real sample artifact would make a skeptical first-year immediately recognize their problem being solved?
- Can every plan’s renewal terms be understood from a screenshot, without calculation?
- If only three sections stayed before pricing, which would demonstrate the product instead of restating its promise?
